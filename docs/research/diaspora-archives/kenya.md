## Recommended additions: primary documents inspected

### 1. A family with births recorded in both Kutch and Nairobi

**Citation:** Kenya Gazette, vol. LXV, no. 21, 7 May 1963, printed p. 522, Gazette Notice 1986, “Notice of Change of Name”. PDF page 24.

**URL:** https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/ke/1963/ke-government-gazette-dated-1963-05-07-no-21.pdf#page=24

**Evidence:** Karsan Keshra, resident in Nairobi, executed a deed poll as father and guardian of three daughters. One daughter's birthplace is Bharasar in Cutch, India, in 1944; the younger daughters were born in Nairobi in 1952 and 1954. The deed was executed in April 1963. Text and actual scanned page were read.

**Short extract (12 words):** “Amarbai, born at Bharasar in Cutch in India in 1944”.

**What it supports:** A named family linking a specific Kutch village and Nairobi across two decades. Women and children appear in the documentary record, rather than only male merchants. These birth locations establish family geography; the notice does not give an arrival date or prove one move between 1944 and 1952. Parents could have travelled repeatedly. Do not infer mother tongue from birthplace or surname.

**Editorial recommendation:** Public prose can refer to the father and the three daughters' birth years/places without reproducing children's names, full birth dates or registration numbers. No arrival arrow or arrival total is justified.

**Access note:** The search service mislabels this result “21/05/1963”, but the PDF masthead and p. 522 both clearly read 7 May 1963. Cite the document, not the search title.

**Files:** `kenya-research/1963-05-07.pdf`, `.txt`, `1963-p522.png`.

### 2. Widow in Mankuva, estate in Nairobi, death in Uganda

**Citation:** Kenya Gazette, vol. LXIII, no. 53, 21 November 1961, printed p. 1440, Gazette Notice **5623**, Nairobi Probate and Administration, Cause 300 of 1961. PDF page 18.

**URL:** https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/ke/1961/ke-government-gazette-dated-1961-11-21-no-53.pdf#page=18

**Evidence:** Gangdas Kanji Patel of Nairobi applied as attorney for Dhankuvar, widow of Mavji Jivraj Patel, residing at Mankuva, Cutch, India. The deceased is described more fully as Mavji Jivraj Vasta Patel of Nairobi; his death is recorded at Kiriri, Uganda, on 6 April 1959. Actual scanned page and text read.

**Short extract (18 words):** “Mavji Jivraj Vasta Patel of Nairobi aforesaid, who died at Kiriri in Uganda”.

**What it supports:** A legally documented family/estate connection spanning Kutch, Kenya and Uganda. It makes the widow's location visible and demonstrates that an estate's geography can extend beyond the deceased's recorded Kenyan residence. It does not establish his birthplace, when either spouse migrated, why he was in Uganda, or that the widow had ever lived in Kenya. The attorney's relationship beyond legal agency is unspecified.

**Correction to initial quick message:** The notice is 5623, not 5622. Gangdas is an attorney; no cousin relationship is stated.

**Files:** `kenya-research/1961-11-21.pdf`, `.txt`, `1961-p1440.png`.

### 3. Mandvi dhow cargo in Mombasa, 1923

**Citation:** Official Gazette of the Colony and Protectorate of Kenya, vol. XXVI, no. 922, 2 January 1924, printed p. 7, General Notice 11, customs auction notice signed at Mombasa on 19 December 1923. PDF page 7.

**URL:** https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/ke/1924/ke-government-gazette-dated-1924-01-02-no-922.pdf#page=7

**Evidence:** The unclaimed cargo table for the King's Warehouse, Mombasa, contains a row dated 22 May 1923 for a dhow from Cutch Mandvi and four loads of red chalk. The scanned name appears **“Fatch Survani”**; OCR gives “Patch Survani”. The primary text was visually checked. A scheduled auction was to occur on 4 February 1924 if goods remained uncleared.

**Short extract (7 words):** “Dhow ‘Fatch Survani’ @ Cutch Mandvi”.

**What it supports:** Continued documented shipping between Mandvi and Mombasa in the railway/steamship era. It is a cargo record, not a passenger manifest; it identifies neither the crew's language nor migrant households. The dates are the table's record dates and should not be embellished as precise passenger arrivals. If concerned about the ship-name glyph, public prose can omit the vessel's name while retaining the route/date.

**Files:** `kenya-research/1924-01-02.pdf`, `.txt`, `1924-p7.png`.

### 4. Mombasa probate administration for a Mandvi estate

**Citation:** Official Gazette of the Colony and Protectorate of Kenya, 21 May 1935, printed p. 563, General Notice 673, Mombasa District Registry, Cause 19 of 1935. **PDF page 67** in the available combined scan.

**URL:** https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/ke/1935/ke-government-gazette-dated-1935-05-21-no-25.pdf#page=67

**Evidence:** Narotam Purushottam of Mombasa applied to administer intestate the estate of Ranchhordas Lalji, late of Cutch-Mandvi, who died in Mandvi on 12 December 1931. Notice dated Mombasa, 10 May 1935. Scanned p. 563 and OCR read.

**Short extract (13 words):** “Narotam Purushottam, of Mombasa, for the administration intestate of the estate”.

**What it supports:** A specific cross-border estate-administration connection. It does **not** establish that Ranchhordas lived in Kenya, that Narotam was a relative, or that either was a Kutchi speaker. Useful research register entry; weaker as evidence of migration than the family birth record.

**Access:** Web open returned 403, but ordinary public PDF download succeeded (4.1 MB). Actual PDF has earlier material before this issue, which explains page 67; printed page is authoritative.

**Files:** `kenya-research/1935-05-21.pdf`, `.txt`, `1935-p563.png`.

### 5. Direct evidence that Cutchi and Gujarati cannot be equated

**Citation:** *Abdulhamisi A. Salyani & others v Rukiyabhai M. Saleh*, [2008] KEHC 1055 (KLR), High Court at Mombasa, Civil Case 692 of 1993, F. Azangalala J., 16 July 2008.

**Official URL:** https://kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/judgment/kehc/2008/1055/eng%402008-07-16

**Accessible full reproduction:** https://sheriahub.com/cases/ke/caselaw/abdulhamisi-a-salyani-others-v-rukiyabhai-m-saleh-2008-eklr

**Locator:** Unnumbered judgment passage beginning “The plaintiffs contended that the defendant acknowledged receipt...”, followed by discussion of the language in which agreements were explained. Search “mother tongue Cutchi”. Full reproduction read; official catalogue/search result verifies neutral citation, court and date. Official full-page retrieval was inaccessible in this tool.

**Short extract (11 words):** “She understands only Kiswahili and her mother tongue Cutchi.”

**What it supports:** A court accepted a Mombasa woman's testimony distinguishing her Cutchi and Kiswahili repertoire from Gujarati and English, with consequences for a property dispute. This adds women's language experience and challenges the assumption that Gujarati documentation is accessible to all Kutchi speakers. The judgment does not supply birthplace, migration history or community-wide language rates. Public discussion need not rehearse personal property details or other relatives' names.
