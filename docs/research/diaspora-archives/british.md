## 1. John Kirk's Mombasa report, 6 November 1873: substantial new evidence

- British Library, India Office Records, **IOR/L/PS/6/115, ff. 65–73a**, enclosure no. 1, letter no. 78 from Political Agent and Consul-General, Zanzibar to Secretary, Government of India Foreign Department, **6 November 1873**. Forwarded with Political Despatch no. 8 of 9 January 1874.
- Main passage: **f. 66r, printed p. 1, image 5/24, paragraphs 7–8**.
- Record: https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100122833548.0x000006
- Catalogue: https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/041-003719634
- Scan: https://iiif.qdl.qa/iiif/images/81055/vdc_100110511460.0x000001/IOR_L_PS_6_115_%20ff%2065-73A_0005.jp2/full/!1800,1800/0/default.jpg
- Local inspected files: `qdl-1874-f66r.html`, `qdl-1873-kirk-f66r.jpg`. The web search fetcher repeatedly failed this record; ordinary curl retrieved its full HTML and the IIIF image. The scan was visually checked.

**What it establishes.** Kirk reports merchants from Mombasa visiting Malindi for about three months during the millet/sesame harvest, then returning to Mombasa where their wives and families lived. He identifies Bohras, Scindis and Hindus. For people he calls Scindis, who called themselves Meman, their chiefs supplied an arrival chronology: first arrivals twenty years previously, initially two houses; most others arrived over the previous seventeen years; thirty houses by the visit. This suggests **approximately 1853**, explicitly as a reported estimate. Kirk connects the group with Kutch and Sindh. His wording about descent and birth is ambiguous about which generation was born in Kutch. Family residence is directly described by 1873; it cannot automatically be backdated to 1853. This is an 1873 document reporting an earlier community history, adding evidence alongside the 1915 litigation.

**Limitations.** Kirk's categories are his own colonial classifications; no home language is recorded. The wording concerning Sindhi descent and birth in Kutch should be quoted or paraphrased cautiously, without claiming an enumerated linguistic population. It is not an exact first arrival register. His political-refugee interpretation and claims about permanent departure are observations, not independently verified motives. Women and families are unnamed; their birthplaces and dates of arrival are not stated.

Short excerpt suitable for publication: “returning to Mombassa where their wives and families reside” (10 words). Avoid adding more than 15 further verbatim words from this source if using that excerpt.

The same letter, **f. 66v, printed p. 2, paragraphs 9–16**, records direct ivory shipment to Bombay, local porters, and four resident Indians in Takaungu among fourteen encountered. These provide context but are less directly Kutch-specific. https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100122833548.0x000007 . Local full HTML `qdl-1874.html` read.

## 2. Zanzibar Agency administrative report for 1870, reproduced by Saldanha in 1906

- Jerome Anthony Saldanha, *Précis on slave trade in the Gulf of Oman and the Persian Gulf, 1873–1905 (With a Retrospect into previous history from 1852)*, printed Simla, **23 June 1906**. British Library **IOR/L/PS/20/C246**.
- **Printed p. 13** expressly introduces an extract from the Administration Report of the Zanzibar Agency, **1870**, with details in annexures 1–3. **Printed p. 14** begins Annexure no. 1. **Printed p. 15** continues it with Bohra population table.
- p. 13: https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100023517342.0x000017
- p. 14: https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100023517342.0x000018
- p. 15: https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100023517342.0x000019
- Scan p. 15: https://iiif.qdl.qa/iiif/images/81055/vdc_100000000884.0x0001cc/IOR_L_PS_20_C246_0024.jp2/full/1800,/0/default.jpg
- Local `qdl-saldanha-p13.html`, `qdl-saldanha-p14.html`, `qdl-1870-bohra-table.jpg`. All relevant text read; p. 15 table visually checked.

The Bohra table, explicitly labelled as 1870 statistics:

| Place as printed | Adult males | Adult females | Children | Total | Houses |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Lamo | 20 | 14 | 8 | 42 | 11 |
| Melinda | 37 | blank | blank | 37 | 21 |
| Mombassa | 39 | 33 | 70 | 142 | 25 |
| Wassein | 2 | blank | blank | 2 | 2 |

**What it establishes.** Administrative documentation explicitly includes women, children and houses at Mombasa and Lamu before 1895. Adjacent prose reports substantial Bohra emigration from Kutch after earlier arrivals from Surat and Rajkot.

**Limits.** These are **Bohra statistics, not a Kutchi census**. Not every recorded person can be assigned a Kutchi origin or language. Houses are not automatically families. No method, individual names or birthplace breakdown accompanies these Kenya rows. Blank cells must not automatically become verified zeroes. This is a 1906 reproduction of statistics labelled 1870; the inspected source chain does not establish who personally enumerated them. No claim of Christie authorship has been established. Separate p. 14 family-origin statistics (432 of 535 from Kutch) refer to **Zanzibar Khoja families**, not Mombasa or Lamu.

Suggested article use: one compact paragraph plus the two strongest Kenya rows, Mombasa and Lamu, with caption preserving date, category and source chain. Total derived discussion should stay below 200 words per source.

## 3. Kazi Shahabudin memorandum, London, 14 February 1870

- British Library **IOR/L/PS/18/B90**, *Slave-dealing and Slave-holding by Kutchees in Zanzibar* (compiled 1870), **ff. 114v–115v, printed pp. 14–16**.
- https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100023800070.0x000012
- https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100023800070.0x000013
- https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100023800070.0x000014
- Local scan of p. 14: `qdl-1870-memo.jpg`, visually checked.

Kazi Shahabudin, identified as Dewan of the Rao of Kutch, reports an unnamed Bhatia trader's observations from 1859–1866. Lamu and Mombasa are named in the port network. Regional origins distinguish Kutch, Bombay, Kathiawar and Portuguese possessions rather than treating Indian merchants as one group. The following page explicitly states that the informant's figures are rough estimates. The memoir also records long absences from India and return movement, but its statements about wives apply to the Hindu traders described, not all migrant communities.

The memorandum is politically situated: its denials/minimisations of participation in slavery should not be accepted as independently established fact. Particularly useful as a named Kutchi official's account of the diaspora and as evidence of the archival argument itself. Less useful as a population census. The main article can prioritise Kirk's specific Mombasa family passage and the 1870 Bohra table rather than overload itself with every overlapping number.

## 4. House of Lords, 22 June 1876: jurisdiction over Kutch subjects abroad

- UK Parliament, Hansard, *India—Slave Trading of Subjects of Native Princes in India—Slave Trade Bill*, first reading, **22 June 1876**.
- https://hansard.parliament.uk/lords/1876-06-22/debates/18dcfaae-2fab-4923-8b17-655ab5720e59/India%E2%80%94SlaveTradingOfSubjectsOfNativePrincesInIndiaSlaveTradeBill

Full text read. Salisbury describes a 1874 Bombay case concerning a subject of the Rao of Cutch charged following the Zanzibar consul's intervention; his speech explains the claimed jurisdictional gap and proposed legislation. Stanley questions the authority to legislate without rulers' consent. This is parliamentary evidence of disputed imperial protection and legal status, relevant to overseas mobility and responsibility. It is not itself a neutral account of the defendant's guilt, or proof that the proposed bill had become law. A useful primary complement to Ibrahim, but not essential to the new settlement chronology.

## 5. House of Commons, 5 July 1906: Mombasa claims for equal rights

- *British Indians in East Africa*, Hansard vol. 160, **5 July 1906**.
- https://hansard.parliament.uk/commons/1906-07-05/debates/05098b66-c6a1-4725-ab5b-b48e5b17833a/BritishIndiansInEastAfrica

Full text read. Churchill reports a meeting by the Indian community in Mombasa requesting equal rights with Europeans, and the settlers' counter-resolution in Nairobi. Useful direct evidence of political agency and conditions of settlement. Broad Indian category; no Kutchi-specific participation established.

## 6. House of Commons, 30 October 1918: Mombasa segregation and residence

- *Mombasa*, Hansard, **30 October 1918**.
- https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1918-10-30/debates/21d0dfde-64df-4427-a00b-ccf2a294966e/Mombasa

Full text read. Wedgwood questions segregating Indians in a special part of Mombasa and the availability of residential land; Hewins acknowledges representations and invokes sanitary considerations. This documents debate, discrimination and official responses. It does not provide Kutchi-specific names, figures or an account of every affected household.

## 7. House of Commons, 29 March 1973: limits of onward-migration statistics

- *Kenyan Asians*, Hansard, **29 March 1973**.
- https://hansard.parliament.uk/%E2%80%8CCommons/1973-03-29/debates/9cda4d29-defe-4d9e-85f4-97eafa877dac/KenyanAsians

Full text read. The reply says available arrival statistics could not separate the requested Kenyan Asian group; 652 UK passport holders admitted for settlement in January–February came from Kenya, Malawi, Tanzania and Zambia together. Do not label that 652 as Kenyans, Asians or Kutchi. This is a useful demonstration of why later migration totals require disaggregation, rather than a new population claim.
