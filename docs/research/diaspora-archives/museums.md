# Museum, heritage and oral-history investigation

Checked 22 October 2026. This is a bounded online search, not an inventory of all museum stores. No institutions or individuals were contacted. Catalogues, published research and inspected text are distinguished below. No new public claim of earliest Kutchi settlement follows from these finds alone.

## Publication candidates

### 1. Janbai's bracelet and portrait, Peabody Essex Museum

- Source actually read: Julia Stephens, 2024, “Material modernities: Tracing Janbai’s gendered mobilities across the Indian Ocean,” Modern Asian Studies 58(2):386–420, DOI 10.1017/S0026749X23000550. Online publication marked 9 October 2024.
- Full text: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/modern-asian-studies/article/material-modernities-tracing-janbais-gendered-mobilities-across-the-indian-ocean/956A9B9FBEF4EED91DA5162C92D01724
- PDF: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/956A9B9FBEF4EED91DA5162C92D01724/S0026749X23000550a.pdf/material_modernities_tracing_janbais_gendered_mobilities_across_the_indian_ocean.pdf
- Actual item identifiers reproduced in figures: bracelet **E22365.A**, portrait **TR2016.13**. Figures 2–3, printed pp.399–400, and surrounding “Between three coasts” section. Museum acquired bracelet from Minnie Batchelder in 1932, with portrait gifted in the same year. Article describes gifts originally made by Janbai to Caroline Batchelder in Zanzibar in the early 1880s, but the detailed family story accompanying the objects has been lost.
- Research explicitly identifies Janbai as Kutchi-speaking and both Janbai's and Tharia Topan's families as originating in Kutch. This is stronger identification than inferring identity from an object style. Article connects a family manuscript biography and legal records with museum objects. It reconstructs Zanzibar–Bombay household movement and burial of a daughter in Aden, not a Kenyan household itinerary.
- Useful addition: named woman's economic agency, material archive and family movement within the wider maritime network; may strengthen the article's section on women missing from merchant histories.
- Limits: direct PEM object catalogue records were not found in public search; accession and provenance are verified through peer-reviewed research, rather than independent inspection of museum accession registers. Do not imply museum objects prove Kenya settlement. Images credited “courtesy PEM”; article CC BY does not automatically establish separate archival-image reuse rights.

### 2. Bhadra Vadgama, Gujarati Yatra oral history, 11 March 2017

- Heritage project source: https://www.subrangarts.co.uk/GujratiYatra/oralhistory.aspx
- Interview metadata: Bhadra Vadgama; interviewers Lata Desai and Rolf Killius; transcription Priya Shah; interview at Ickenham residence on 11 March 2017.
- Published abstract states Kutch ancestry, childhood in Zanzibar, marriage and settlement as a teacher in Kenya, followed by relocation to Britain. This is a named woman's family route, rather than an anonymous regional claim.
- The published curator's synopsis was read. Linked complete interview https://www.youtube.com/embed/bxlfgC3e3y8 could not be retrieved (throttled). Therefore describe as **interview catalogue summary consulted**, not full interview listened to or transcript analysed. No exact migration years should be inferred from her Croydon residence 1977–1999.
- Same heritage project records Edward Simpson interview 28 February 2017 on Badela connections between Kutch and Mombasa, and Arunkant Shah interview 10 May 2017 recounting grandparents' dhow departures in 1899, but Arunkant's origins are only Gujarat in this summary. These do not independently establish Kutchi identity or the first migration. Project's Kanji Malam narrative is not a verified early-arrival claim.
- Further family routes, only Gujarati identification: https://www.subrangarts.co.uk/GujratiYatra/oralhistory2.aspx (Shantilal Dudakia Madagascar–Aden–India–Mombasa–Britain1968; Maheshkumar Kavi Bhavnagar–Mombasa by SS Karanja, joined by wife and children, Britain1971). Useful comparisons, not Kutchi counts.

### 3. Harar manuscript with a Diu endowment notice

- Sana Mirza, 15 April 2025, “From Harar to Diu: Circulation and Reception of a Qur’anic Manuscript across the Indian Ocean,” Journal18, issue19 Africa.
- Full article inspected: https://www.journal18.org/19-africa/from-harar-to-diu-circulation-and-reception-of-a-quranic-manuscript-across-the-indian-ocean/
- Physical primary item: Princeton University Library, **Islamic Manuscripts, Third Series no.900q**; catalogue **99125500439306421**, https://catalog.princeton.edu/catalog/99125500439306421 (link timed out in web retrieval; not falsely claimed inspected). Princeton acquired it in2022 from N.G.McBurney, previously Swiss private collection per author and dealer.
- Figures1,5,6 and first paragraphs: material analysis places manuscript in late18th-century Harar. A later endowment note names Hasan/Hassan ibn Adojo, his mother Fatima Shashu and Diu in Gujarat. Exact production date and route remain uncertain. A later date1105/1693–94 is judged unreliable by Mirza because its ink, hand and stylistic evidence differ; **do not adopt1693 as date**.
- Value: material evidence of Ethiopia–western India connections and African-made objects moving eastwards. This reverses an exclusively India-to-Africa story.
- Limits: not an identified Kutchi migrant or direct person-level migration record. Author raises possibility a Harari scribe worked elsewhere. Proposed Zeila/Arabia itinerary is a reasoned reconstruction, not explicit passenger itinerary. A short qualified paragraph would fit Ethiopian context; do not plot as verified migration.

### 4. Gulzar Waljee collection, Surrey History Centre / Migration Museum

- Scholarly institutional profile inspected: https://southasianbritain.org/people/gulzar-waljee/
- Collection **Z/748**, photograph **Z/748/2**, Surrey History Centre. Image caption identifies her outside Royal Surrey County Hospital in1962; photographs reproduced by permission of custodian and donor, not automatically reusable.
- Profile records Tanga1940 birth, Britain1959 nursing training, return to work in Nairobi before returning to Britain1967. It identifies father from Kutch, but also gives1890 arrival combined with Uganda–Kenya railway construction. Since construction began1896, this concatenation deserves checking and **should not become a precise family migration date**.
- Direct first-person institutional account inspected: Royal Surrey NHS, Stefan Grant17July2024, https://www.royalsurrey.nhs.uk/news/84yearold-gulzar-reflects-on-embarking-her-career-as-an-international-midwife-in-the-uk-12880/ . It confirms1959 training and Gujarat origins, but notKutch or father's1890date.
- Museum exhibit URL: https://heartofthenation.migrationmuseum.org/arrival/gulzar-waljee/ (502, not inspected).
- Surrey custody corroboration: https://www.surreycc.gov.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/346292/Surrey-History-Centre-accessions-2022.pdf and https://www.surreyarchaeology.org.uk/sites/default/files/22SurreyHistory2023.pdf (search records identifyZ/748 and donated narrative/photographs). Useful precisely located future retrieval lead; weaker immediate article addition than Vadgama or Janbai.

### British Museum, Kaderdina textiles and Lamu printing blocks

- Institution-authored exhibition inspected: https://artsandculture.google.com/story/twWxNsyPc0L4Iw?hl=en “Social Fabric: African Textiles Today,” curated by Chris Spring.
- Explicitly names the Kaderdina family textile business in Biashara Street, Mombasa, identifies Indian heritage, and describes African women's design preferences and locally carved Swahili printing blocks. No Kutch origin demonstrated here. Good commercial context, insufficient migration evidence.
- BM **Af2002,09.5**, https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Af2002-09-5 : printed kanga, production India (question mark), Tanzania field collection in November 2002. Search record only. Manufacture and acquisition place should not be converted into migrant identity.

### Harar museum-house evidence

- S. Tarsitani 2009, “Merging Past and Present in the Museums of Harar, Ethiopia,” Nilo-Ethiopian Studies 13:1–16. Full PDF read: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/niloethiopian/2009/13/2009_1/_pdf . Printed p. 8 (RCC section) and p. 15 note 12 identify a c.1890 house and Indian merchant Jivagee Nazarela via Revault and Santelli 2004 p.229. It also says the house was built a few years after Rimbaud left in 1891, so the construction date is insufficiently consistent for precision. No Kutchi identity. The existing article's Harar photograph appropriately avoids identifying an exact builder.
- Mirza 2017, “The visual resonances of a Harari Qur'an,” https://doi.org/10.4000/afriques.2052 , full text indexed. QUR706 in the Khalili Collection was copied in Shawwal1162/September–October1749; later ownership notes include Zanzibar1839. The source shows biḥārī artistic connections, not ethnicity. Useful counterexample to architectural or stylistic identity inference.

## Institutional search coverage and unresolved leads

- National Museums of Kenya's official Nairobi National Museum pages list the Asian African Heritage Gallery: https://museums.or.ke/nairobi-national-museum/ and https://nmk.go.ke/nairobi-national-museum/ . Found exhibition metadata, but no public itemwise Kutchi immigration registers or person-level accession entries.
- Designer's account https://www.openhandstudios.org/project/kenya/ says the temporary 2000 exhibition used artifacts and photographs contributed by 100 volunteers, with a permanent exhibition agreement in 2010. It gives an institutional retrieval trail, rather than independent primary proof of each historical claim displayed.
- Family page https://pattniconnection.com/History/MuslimParajiya.html names Noor Mohamed Haji Elias as the great-grandfather whose photograph descendant Mohammed Zakariya says was in Nairobi National Museum, and identifies the Cutchi Muslim Sonara Jamat. No accession ID, date or custody verification, so an **unverified family provenance lead**. Do not adopt its population estimates.
- Leicester EMOHA catalogue guide https://le.ac.uk/emoha/themes/british-empire-commonwealth/leicester-oral-history-collection identifies India–Nairobi–England1967 recording **866, LO/230/181**, Kenya/Uganda–Leicester **911, LO/266/217**, East Africa–Leicester1966 **886, LO/250/201**. Old item links redirect to the homepage; recording contents were not inspected and regional identity remains unknown. https://le.ac.uk/emoha/collections/south-asian describes series EMOHA/20,69,17,96, etc. These are future catalogue requests, not new facts about Kutchi arrival.
- Searches of V&A, Pitt Rivers, Royal Museums Greenwich and museum web records did not yield a new public item record proving Kutchi migration to Kenya or Ethiopia. RMG coastal charts identify Kutch geography. This online search does not establish an absence of evidence in the collections themselves.
- Crucial false positive: BM taxonomy **x90101**, https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/term/x90101, lists “Kutchi” as a variant of **Kuchi, semi-nomadic people of Afghanistan**. Its dress2008,6039.1 and shoeAs1966,01.619.a-b are irrelevant to Kachchh. Read classification scope notes before aggregating by keyword.

## Suggested restrained public update

Add Janbai's named museum objects as a women's history and method example within the Zanzibar context; Bhadra Vadgama's dated interview catalogue as a qualified Kenyan onward-migration case; and Mirza's 2025 manuscript study as evidence of material circulation in both directions in the Ethiopian context. Keep new historical claims brief and qualified by their sources. Preserve the full search ledger for future retrieval. These museum finds do not move the earliest documented Kenyan Kutchi arrival date.
