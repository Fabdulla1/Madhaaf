# European archives investigation: Kutch, Kenya and Ethiopia

Research checked 22 October 2026. This is a bounded online investigation, not a claim that every European archive has been searched. Distinguish EU institutions, the Council of Europe and national European collections.

## Verified primary source: Charles Guillain's French expedition

Charles Guillain, *Documents sur l'histoire, la géographie et le commerce de l'Afrique orientale*, deuxième partie, tome deuxième (third physical volume), Paris: Arthus Bertrand, 1856. Expedition of 1846–1848. The volume's narrative explicitly dates May 1848 before its Mombasa stay. Digitised Smithsonian Libraries copy, Internet Archive identifier `Documentssurlqut318Guil`, mirrored on Wikimedia Commons.

### Finding 1: resident merchants and temporary visitors in Mombasa

Printed p. 238, PDF p. 248. Reports about fifty Indian residents classified as banians and hénoud, alongside other Indians temporarily staying for trade. This distinction supports careful treatment of residence versus seasonal visits. It does not identify every resident as Kutchi; this is an observer's estimate, not a census. Its criticism of Indian competition with Swahili merchants is the author's interpretation.

### Finding 2: specific Kutch-linked shipping at Mombasa

Printed p. 267, PDF p. 277; original scan visually checked. Discusses Mombasa's ivory exports and two ships, together approximately 300 kandi, owned by merchants of Kutch and Bombay, departing for India at the start of the southwest monsoon. A third vessel belonging to Syed Khaled carried remaining goods at the end of the monsoon. This is direct Kutch-linked Mombasa commerce before 1895. Ownership does not establish a family settlement or each sailor's regional identity.

### Finding 3: commercial residence through agents

Printed pp. 372–373, PDF pp. 382–383; both original scans visually checked. Describes Zanzibar Banyan merchants as mostly linked to large Kutch houses and Bombay houses; their agents stayed at Lamu, Mombasa and other coastal centres for two or three years, then were replaced by other Zanzibar employees. This adds an intermediate form of migration: rotating commercial residence. The origin of every agent is not supplied. The author reports having been told that this coastal residence pattern had existed for about twenty years; that is retrospective testimony, not an exact first-arrival date. Do not turn it into a secure 1828 founding date.

### Finding 4: named merchant's origin and political status

Printed p. 388, PDF p. 398. Identifies the customs farmer Djiram as a native of Kutch under British consular protection, although ostensibly a subject of Saïd. This corroborates a named regional origin and layered jurisdiction in Zanzibar; it does not establish Djiram's residence in Kenya. His identification with Jairam Shivji requires independent name matching. The surrounding hostile characterization is the French author's judgment.

## Verified official European human-rights record

European Commission of Human Rights, *East African Asians v. the United Kingdom*, applications 4403/70 and others, report adopted 14 December 1973, made public by resolution of 21 March 1994. This is a Council of Europe record, not an EU institution or the European Commission of the EU.

Official record: https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/eng?i=001-73658

Official scanned PDF: https://hudoc.echr.coe.int/app/conversion/pdf/?id=001-73658&library=ECHR

Downloaded full 65-page official scan. OCR intermediates under `echr-ocr/`. Headnote and individual printed p. 42 visually checked; conclusion pages OCR read directly. It describes racial discrimination in admission restrictions and their relationship to degrading treatment under Article 3. This is a Commission report, not a Court judgment.

PDF p. 38, printed p. 42, paras. 65–67: individual Kenya-born applicants H.V. Shah (application 4409/70), N.K.D.S. Shah (4410/70), and L.A. Rawal (4411/70). Initials H.V. verified visually, correcting poor OCR. Para. 65 describes H.V. Shah, born Kenya 18 December 1951, citizen of the UK and Colonies, arrival in Britain 24 January 1970, refusal and Canterbury Prison detention until 9 March, followed by permanent admission in June 1971. This shows an individual onward-migration experience. No Kutchi identity is stated, and surnames cannot provide it.

Printed p. 62 / PDF p. 58, paras. 207–209: the Commission finds the racial discrimination amounted to degrading treatment and concludes Article 3 was violated, by six votes to three. This applies to the 25 citizen-of-UK-and-Colonies applicants under consideration in that subsection. Printed p. 63 / PDF p. 59, paras. 210–212, explains that all 25 eventually obtained permanent admission but that this did not retroactively eliminate the earlier violation.

Critical qualification: para. 213 on printed p. 63, continuing at paras. 214–215 on printed p. 64 / PDF p. 60, distinguishes the six British protected persons. The Commission finds no Article 3 violation in those cases because their entry position was not altered by the 1968 Act and the relevant legislation did not distinguish racial groups of protected persons. Do not describe the report as finding an Article 3 violation for all 31 applicants. N.K.D.S. Shah and L.A. Rawal belong to this distinct protected-person group.

## European searches and remaining leads

- EU institutions: searched Europeana, Historical Archives of the European Union/EUI and Archives Portal Europe with Kutchi, Cutchi, Kenyan Asians, Kenya and Indian variants. No directly relevant Kutchi migration primary record was established. Direct search pages on Europeana and EUI could not be read through the web tool. Do not interpret this as proof their holdings contain nothing relevant.
- French collections: searched Gallica/BnF and ANOM/colonial Djibouti variants (Cutch, Keutch, Koutch, Indiens, Djibouti, Mombase). Guillain was recovered through digitised national-library/Smithsonian copies rather than a readable Gallica delivery. ANOM catalogue-level discovery did not establish individual Kutchi migrants in this pass.
- Italian collections: official national cultural-property catalogue item `0100405205`, https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0100405205, concerns a sabre marked Mohamedally & Co and links it to Ethiopia. Page retrieval timed out; indexed dating 1820–1830 appears inconsistent with the firm's late nineteenth-century history. It is an unresolved material-culture lead, not migration proof.
- German/Portuguese searches did not yield a new specific Kutchi passenger, consular or household record in the retrieved results. Existing Roteiro evidence must retain its Indian/Gujarati identity limitations.
- A promising Portuguese retrieval resource emerged: Matheus Serva Pereira et al., ‘African and Asian Writings from Mozambique: Uncovering Indigenous Records in a Portuguese Colonial Archive’, *History in Africa* (2025), https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/history-in-africa/article/african-and-asian-writings-from-mozambique-uncovering-indigenous-records-in-a-portuguese-colonial-archive/24A1DA2E74FB77E279E7757C524FB5A4 . Full scholarly text read. It describes the INDICO catalogue, https://indico.ics.ulisboa.pt/database/ , of indigenous documents in the Arquivo Histórico Ultramarino's Mozambique series, 1752–1833. Relevant original holdings include box 266, documents 119 and 125, bilingual collective representations with Gujarati and Arabic signatures, and box 25, document 57, Portuguese correspondence about a Mombasa ambassador. The article also identifies travel licence applications. These are excellent future retrieval paths for indigenous merchant voices, but the documents examined here do not establish Kutchi identity or Kenya household settlement. No uninspected holding is adopted as a finding.
- No institutions or individuals were contacted, no restricted collections bypassed, and no claims were based solely on catalogue titles.
